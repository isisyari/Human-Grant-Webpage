About the HUMAN Initiative

A $1.2 million Mellon Foundation initiative exploring what it means to be human in the age of AI, with emphasis on equity, justice, and meaningful human-AI collaboration.

1.1
Mission

The Grant

The Krebs Center for the Humanities is the convener of a $1.2 million grant from the Mellon Foundation for the HUMAN (Humanities Understanding of the Machine-Assisted Nexus) project.

This multi-year initiative aims to explore artificial intelligence through a humanities perspective, equipping students with the skills to ethically integrate AI into their professional lives with an emphasis on equity and justice.

HUMAN explores what it means to be human in the age of AI by engaging an interdisciplinary group of humanities faculty fellows who, in partnership with Chicago-based organizations, are developing new courses, digital humanities research projects, seminars, publications, artist residencies, and other means for students to gain a broad understanding of the role that the humanities should play in public policy, cultural preservation, and community education in an AI-inflected world.

HUMAN Fellows Program

The HUMAN Fellows program is an opportunity for scholars to explore the intersection of humanities and artificial intelligence through a lens of equity and justice. The program recognizes the potential for unchecked AI to exacerbate social inequities and disproportionately impact marginalized populations, and seeks to address these concerns by supporting projects that engage in humanistic practices while deploying AI technologies.

Each year, up to seven HUMAN Fellows are selected to propose either a new "AI and humanities" project or work on an existing HUMAN project. Fellows develop courses, research initiatives, and publications that critically examine AI's role in society.
