AI in Higher Education

Academic research and scholarly perspectives on artificial intelligence in education, featuring peer-reviewed papers and expert analysis.

HUMAN Digest · March 2026
2.1

Assessing Deep Learning: A Work Program for the Humanities

AI Ethics Journal Peer-Reviewed

Authors: Segessenmann, J., Stadelmann, T., Davison, A. et al.
Published in: AI Ethics 5, 1-32 (2025)

"While AI is used regularly now, it is not really understood by everyone." From the paper's key findings

Key Insights

This comprehensive paper examines how the humanities can and should engage with artificial intelligence in the age of deep learning. The authors argue for a more nuanced understanding of AI systems beyond their surface-level applications.

Why It Matters

As AI becomes ubiquitous in academic settings, this work program provides a framework for humanities scholars to critically assess and engage with these technologies rather than simply adopting or rejecting them wholesale.

  • DOI: 10.1007/s43681-023-00408-z (Full Paper)
2.2

Engineering Equity: Designing Diversity-Aware AI

AI & Society 2025

Author: Shin, D.
Published in: AI & Society (2025)

This article highlights the critical importance of incorporating diversity in AI systems and the ethical imperative to design technology that reflects all of humanity.

"The necessity to create diversity-aware AI stems from the understanding that if AI is to mimic human intelligence in meaningful ways, it may surpass static, monolithic models that narrowly reflect only a portion of the human experience." Shin, D.

Core Arguments

  • AI systems often perpetuate existing biases when trained on non-representative data
  • Diversity-aware design is not just ethical but also produces better, more robust AI
  • The humanities and social sciences have crucial roles to play in AI development
  • Engineering teams must incorporate diverse perspectives from the ground up
  • DOI: 10.1007/s00146-025-02314-w (Full Paper)
Why This Matters
2.3

The Humanities' Role in AI Development

The research highlighted in this digest underscores a critical gap in how AI is currently developed and deployed. While technical capabilities advance rapidly, our collective understanding and ethical frameworks often lag behind.

Key Takeaways for Educators

  • Critical Assessment: Students need frameworks to evaluate AI systems, not just use them
  • Interdisciplinary Approach: AI literacy requires perspectives from philosophy, sociology, and the arts
  • Equity Focus: Understanding bias in AI is essential for all graduates entering the workforce
  • Active Engagement: Humanities scholars should shape AI development, not just respond to it

For Further Exploration

These papers represent just the beginning of academic engagement with AI. We encourage readers to explore the full texts and consider how these insights apply to their own disciplines and teaching practices.
