John Tasioulas

Director, Institute for Ethics in AI · University of Oxford

John Tasioulas is a Greek-Australian moral and legal philosopher who serves as the inaugural Director of the Institute for Ethics in AI and Professor of Ethics and Legal Philosophy in the Faculty of Philosophy at the University of Oxford.

"The question is not whether AI will transform society, but whether we can ensure that transformation serves human flourishing." John Tasioulas

Why He Matters

As one of the world's leading thinkers on AI ethics, Tasioulas brings a philosopher's rigor to questions that technologists often approach pragmatically. His work bridges the gap between abstract ethical principles and the concrete challenges of AI governance.

Areas of Focus

  • The nature of human rights in the age of AI
  • Ethical frameworks for autonomous systems
  • The philosophy of AI decision-making
  • Governance and regulation of artificial intelligence

Why Ethics in AI Matters
3.2

The Institute for Ethics in AI

The Institute for Ethics in AI at the University of Oxford brings together world-leading philosophers with experts in other fields, including computer science, law, politics, and economics, to study and help shape the future of AI for the benefit of humanity.

Research Themes

  • Foundations: What ethical principles should govern AI development?
  • Governance: How should AI systems be regulated?
  • Impact: How does AI affect human autonomy, privacy, and dignity?
  • Futures: What kind of AI-integrated society do we want to create?

Following the work of scholars like Tasioulas helps us understand not just what AI can do, but what it should do, and how we can ensure technology serves human values rather than undermining them.

Exploring AI through the humanities with emphasis on equity and justice.
