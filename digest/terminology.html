Understanding AI: A Glossary

Navigating discussions about artificial intelligence requires familiarity with key terminology. This glossary provides clear, accessible definitions for essential AI concepts.

Foundational Concepts

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems. These processes include learning (acquiring information and rules), reasoning (using rules to reach conclusions), and self-correction.

Machine Learning (ML)

A subset of AI where systems learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed. ML algorithms build models based on training data to make predictions or decisions.

Deep Learning

A subset of machine learning using artificial neural networks with multiple layers. Deep learning excels at recognizing patterns in unstructured data like images, text, and audio.

Neural Network

Computing systems inspired by biological neural networks in human brains. They consist of interconnected nodes (neurons) that process information using connectionist approaches.

Generative AI

Large Language Model (LLM)

AI models trained on vast amounts of text data that can understand and generate human-like text. Examples include GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini.

Generative AI

AI systems capable of creating new content, including text, images, music, and code. These systems learn patterns from training data and generate novel outputs.

Prompt

The input or instruction given to an AI system to generate a response. Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting effective prompts to get desired outputs.

Hallucination

When an AI model generates information that appears plausible but is factually incorrect or fabricated. A significant challenge in deploying AI for factual applications.

AI Ethics & Governance

Algorithmic Bias

Systematic and repeatable errors in AI systems that create unfair outcomes, often reflecting historical biases present in training data or design decisions.

Explainability (XAI)

The degree to which humans can understand how an AI system reaches its decisions. Also known as interpretability, it's crucial for trust and accountability.

AI Alignment

Ensuring AI systems' goals and behaviors match human values and intentions. A central concern in AI safety research.

Responsible AI

The practice of designing, developing, and deploying AI with good intention to empower people and businesses, while fairly impacting society.

Technical Terms

Training Data

The dataset used to teach a machine learning model. The quality and diversity of training data significantly impacts model performance and fairness.

Fine-tuning

The process of taking a pre-trained model and training it further on a specific dataset to specialize its capabilities for particular tasks.

Inference

When a trained AI model makes predictions or generates outputs based on new input data. The operational phase after training.

Token

The basic unit of text processed by language models. A token can be a word, part of a word, or punctuation mark. Models have limits on tokens they can process.
